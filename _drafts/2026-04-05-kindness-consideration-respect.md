These values sound so simple. After all who would claim they do not approach things with kindness, consideration, or respect. But honestly they are anything but simple. They contradict each other in dynamic and complex ways. Each value even contradicts itself when time horizon is taken into consideration.

My goal is talk about how these values, that I have spoken about in conferences, have a really deep intersections and why those intersections matters.

## Definitions ##

Before we can talk about how kindness, consideration, and respect interact we need to approach a shared understanding. So I will define these values so we can talk about them later.

### Kindness ###

Kindness is valuing the relief or prevention of suffering. This comes from a point of caring and that caring desires to protect someone from harm.

#### Kindness in the Moment ####

Acting in Kindness prevents us from doing something that causes suffering. But that is too simple, it also drives us to prevent circumstances that lead to suffering. Even that is not enough, because kindness shows us how to act to minimize suffering that is already happening.

Kindness keeps me aware of the situation I do not tease a stranger, or a friend who is not in the state of mind to enjoy it. Kindness is what drives me to order food for an associate that just returned from the hospital. Kindness keeps me from giving critically focused feedback to a person who I think wont want it.

#### Future-Focused Kindness ####

Acting in Kindness with a view on the future is a different thing entirely. Here you are focused on future suffering not suffering in the now. Because of having a long term view of the situation your actions will be different. You may do something that increases suffering now but ultimately helps a person avoid more suffering in the future.

Acting in kindness here has me giving critically focused feedback to a person. It has me offering to help teach someone even if I think the offer will hurt their pride. It has me show up with a lap top to a friend's home and tell them that because they are unemployed I am going to teach them to program, and they cannot dissuade me.


#### Kindness is Deep ####

##### Kindness Thinks its Superior #####

Kindness is deeply related to the desire to protect and help someone. There are problems with the desires of protection and help. Protection assumes that you are better then the person because you can weather whatever it is you are protecting them from. You can defeat whatever the threat is where the person you are protecting cannot. To show kindness in the moment by not giving critical feedback because you _believe_ the person can not take it right now. You are judging them incapable. You are better then them because you _know_ that they cannot take the information.

This is not to say the desire to protect is 100% bad. There are legitimate times when you do need to protect someone and it is a true act of kindness. When you choose not to jest with a friend who is in a bad mood, might be just what they need. The problem with the desire to protect is that it becomes easy to always protect, or protect when protection is not needed/or wanted (discussed more later).

So what is wrong with a desire to help? Helping is tricky, and full of social connotations. To help someone is a direct statement of superiority. To give someone a dollar to help them buy something at the store directly says, "I can afford it, and you cannot." The implications of help are the one I see most often fumbled over as people seem most unaware of its meaning.

Again, not all help is unwarranted or unwanted. The problem arises when help is given and not wanted or needed. This becomes kindness for vanity sake. A friend of mine gave me a term I love and use often that describes how this feels to the recipient: "Inflict Help", or "Inflicted Help". You "Inflict Help" by giving it to someone who does not want it. Just like you inflict damage.

So the trick with help, is finding the right time to give, and ensure you are giving it for the right reasons. Kindness given for vanity sake is not real kindness.

##### Kindness vs Kindness #####

It is easy to see that not all kindness is the same. Some kindness focuses on the here and now. A different kind of kindness focuses on future outcomes. Kindness comes with a risk of looking like you believe you are better the the person you are showing kindness to.

When looking at the two different time horizons there is real risk in favoring one time horizon over another. If you are always focused on the future, then you appear controlling and unkind. You manipulate the person rather then being a helpful friend. You do not care for who they are only for what you can make them.

When you focus only on the immediate needs then you do not engage with the person in a whole way. You are superficially kind and do not show deep care for what would actually prevent harm. You feel avoidant rather then controlling. You do not fully engage in who the person is. This makes you feel selfish and callous.

If you focus solely on kindness, you appear to help out of obligation of position, desire to control, or because you feel the person is not capable. The intent is lost because you cannot be trusted by the individual you are showing kindness to.

### Consideration ###

Consideration is valuing the persons needs and wants before your own. This is coming from a perspective that what you do effects a person and a desire to not negatively effect that person.

#### Consideration in the Moment ####

Acting with consideration is about taking into account how your actions will effect another persons being. So you act to minimize your impact on them, which means that sometimes you choose an outcome that is less favorable to you to support them.

Consideration is what makes me offer my chair when someone enters a room where there are no more chairs, or refuses the chair that is prospered. It is the reason I consider the rules of etiquette before eating my food in a group.

#### Future-Focused Consideration ####

Acting with consideration is still about taking into account how your actions will effect another, but the perspective changes. The question isn't "How will my actions inconvenience someone now?" The question becomes "How will my actions have lasting impact on someone?"

Consideration has me watching someone fail at something I could just do, because I see they are learning with the failure. It has me intentionally making a mistake to help someone else learn to correct me when I make a real one. It also has me push someone into public speaking knowing it will advance their career. It has me giving a team member I am going to fire because they don't fit in with the team 3 months where they can spend half their day looking for new work.

#### The Other Consideration ####

There is one more aspect of consideration that is worth talking about. This is the one that leads people to approach the work they do with craft. It is consideration, with a long term view, to work for both the best outcomes, and at the highest quality. The thing is this is really the same consideration. It is prioritizing others needs over your own, or even over a different person's.

#### Consideration is Layered ####

##### Consideration Thinks it Knows Better #####

Consideration has an insidious side. It's goal is to fulfill the needs of others. This means that you _know_ the needs of others. And that you are positioned to give them their needs when they are not. It has ties to a kind of arrogance that can only be measured in how small others are. And worse than that, it easily blind the person who suffers from the arrogance, after all they are the ones giving up their resources to help another.

That brings us to the problem with help, again. A relationship that is not balanced between individuals is poisonous to both. The martyr needs someone to be the person for whom they suffer. In the book [Leadership and Self Deception](https://a.co/d/06XyAmqx) the Abinger Institute writes a lot about a state they call collusion. Collusion is where 2 people act in ways that trigger each other, but subconsciously encourage each other to act in those ways. Consideration, when not done out of genuine altruism easily leads to this state.

This does not mean that being considerate is arrogant. It means that consideration can hide behind arrogance. It can be twisted by it, or even morph into it if the person giving it does not carefully monitor themselves.

##### Consideration vs Consideration #####

Consideration is such a powerful thing. It can be humbling to experience when it is perceived as being given genuinely. But it can morph, or come from a place that is not honest. It can look very different when given from the perspective of the future vs right now. In fact, the time perspective can force difficult decision.

Right now is helpful. It is taking care of the person's need in the immediate. Offering someone your chair is a gesture that states you see them. Without the right now, people will not feel seen or heard or cared for.

However, we live beyond the right now. Our lives continue on, and our choices right now may have impact into the future. Showing consideration looking forward in time is how you communicate that someone's well being actually matters. That they are part of the community.

If either falters, and one time horizon is favored, so much is lost. If we only apply consideration in the moment, we will appear nice but uncaring. People do not feel the push from us that shows we truly consider them. If we do not apply consideration in the moment, people do not feel seen. If we do not apply consideration focused on the future we tell others they are not part of our tribe.

##### Consideration vs Kindness #####

Consideration doesn't fail always on its own. Other values contribute to failure as they interact. Consideration and Kindness seem to go hand in hand. One strives to reduce or prevent suffering while the other strives to meet someone's needs. Isn't reduction of suffering a need?

Well yes, they do compliment each other in so many ways. But what happens when someone's behavior is negatively effecting the team? Say that person is someone everyone loves.

When thinking only in perspective of the person we can have all these waring views from each value. Consideration in the moment asks: "What is going on now that might be contributing and how can I help?" Consideration in the long term asks: "They might be hurting their relationships, what can I do to highlight that fact?" Kindness in the moment says: "There is no real harm yet, acting will cause harm." Kindness focused on the future says: "Harm is being done in small ways, as the person loses trust of the team. To stop this I need to stop this action."

There are many more ways these values can interact in this scenario. That is the point. Consideration and Kindness can and often conflict. The conflict isn't a bug, it is a feature of having the values, and highlights areas where more care needs to be taken, and where simple answers are nonexistent.

### Respect ###

Respect is valuing the idea that someone has inherent value, and mastery over themselves.

#### Respect in the Moment ####

Respect is about valuing that someone is their own master. They have control over their wants and needs, and it is their decisions that rule them. So respect in the moment is really about allowing someone to be themselves, to act the way they see fit.

Resect directs me to allow someone to leave a work space to get some air and recenter. It listens when a coworker states they need alone time to think. It also recognizes that some people think silently, so I do not need to prod them for being silent. Respect also forces me to accept when someone makes a bad decision or when a coworker rushes a job not taking the standard amount of care and craft. Respect also forces me to acknowledge that other people have different priorities and learn different skills then those I hold valuable.

#### Future-Focused Respect ####

Respect _is_ about valuing someone's control over their sphere of influence. So when viewing out into the future, respect is accepting who they want to be, what they want to be doing.

Here respect, has me firing someone because their working values don't match the teams so that they can find a place to work the way they want to work. It has me inquiring about their actions and what their intent is. It has me stopping them, when they do something that contradicts this whish, and calling them on it even if it is awkward.

#### Respect is Rich ####

##### Respect is Distant #####

To value some's innate control over their own world, you must not interfere. You have to leave them alone, and accept who they are and what they do. At its extreme it is isolating. A person who never interacts with another can claim they are respecting them in the moment. A person who never questions someone's goals and plans can claim they are respecting a person with a view of the future. After all this is what they say they want. So you want to rob some place, go ahead, just don't involve me.

Notice the word _claim_ in the above sentences. That word is doing a lot. It distinguishes between respect and apathy. This one is a dangerous place to be because on the surface apathy can look a lot like respect. Even to ourselves. But the difference is about value. If I value your ability to control your own destiny then I respect you. If I appreciate that you control your own destiny ... over there... probably apathy.

##### Respect vs Respect #####

The differences between future and momentary respect are subtle, but feel very different. I can respect the choices you are making right now, even if they are not choices I would make. I can challenge those choices based on what you claim to want for yourself. If you say you are on a diet and order a huge slice of chocolate cake, I might question you, or even challenge you. Both are respect.

It is the balancing of these view points that gives the layers to respect. You are torn between inaction and challenge. Confrontation, or silent support. Focusing only on the future feels callous, cold and distant because all you do is call out what the person is doing wrong. Where focusing on the moment makes someone feel invisible, uncared for, and isolated because there is no interaction or feedback.

##### Respect vs Kindness #####

Out of all the values Respect and Kindness feel the most alike. Both future view and focusing on the moment have actions that are very similar.

When I am respecting you in the moment, I am not questioning your decisions. When I am kind in the moment I am not providing feedback, but instead trying to take care of your emotions. Both feel like a kind of inaction. In a group, you say you are not going to study tonight for a test tomorrow because you want to see a movie. I respect your ability to make that choice so I don't say or do anything. I don't want to cause you embarrass you by pointing out that you told me that you are struggling in the class.

When I respect you with view on the future, I am questioning and challenging on actions that are contrary to your stated goals. When I am kind to you with the same temporal lense I am giving you crucial feedback on things that might cause you pain. When you announce that you are not going to study, respect that you need this class to get the degree you want, so I question your choice. I know that if you fail the test you will feel like a failure because of the challenges you have been having so I show kindness and and remind you of that.

##### Respect vs Consideration #####

Consideration and Respect feel the most opposite. It is consideration to offer aid to someone, and respect to allow them their choice. These two values seem to conflict at every level.

In the moment consideration is about helping and giving someone what they need. Respect is about giving someone the space to make their own choice. I see that you have taken a task that is outside your current understanding. If I respect that choice, I just accept it. If I am considerate I would inform you that the choice you picked might be beyond you.

Future focused respect is accepting someone's future desires. Where future focused consideration is about recognizing that those desires have outcomes, costs, and effort. So these conflict easily enough, as respect demands that you accept those desires and let the person act on them. Consideration is about questioning those desires or providing aid to achieve them. When you choose the task that is outside your understanding, I respect that this task does not align with your stated work goals and and ask you about it, challenge you about if you really want that task. If I am considerate, I would offer to work with you to help you gain the knowledge required.
